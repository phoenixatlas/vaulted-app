Stablecoins vs bank transfers for cross-border payments

What stablecoins are, how a stablecoin remittance rail works compared with SWIFT correspondent banking, and the benefits and risks to understand.

What is a stablecoin?

A stablecoin is a digital token designed to hold a steady value, usually one US dollar. Regulated stablecoins such as USDC are issued by companies that hold reserves backing each token.

How a traditional international transfer works

A bank wire typically travels through the SWIFT messaging network and one or more correspondent banks. Each hop can add fees and time, and transfers often only move during banking hours.

How a stablecoin rail works

  1. On-ramp: your pounds (or naira, shillings, cedis) are converted to a stablecoin.
  2. Transfer: the stablecoin moves on a blockchain network, 24/7, typically settling in seconds to minutes.
  3. Off-ramp: a local partner converts it to the recipient’s currency and pays their bank or mobile-money wallet.

With a fiat-first app, the sender and recipient only ever see their own currencies.

Potential benefits

  • Faster settlement, including evenings and weekends.
  • Fewer intermediaries, which can mean lower costs.
  • Transparent, traceable movement on-chain.

Risks to understand

  • Stablecoin risk: a token can lose its peg if reserves or the issuer fail.
  • Partner risk: on- and off-ramp partners must be reliable and licensed.
  • Regulation: rules for cryptoassets and payments are evolving; in the UK, cryptoasset promotions carry FCA risk warnings.
  • Cryptoassets held in your own wallet are generally not covered by deposit protection.

How Vaulted uses stablecoins

Vaulted plans to route payments over USDC and low-cost networks such as Stellar, the XRP Ledger, Polygon, Base and Arbitrum, choosing the cheapest route automatically. See corridors and the reverse corridor.
